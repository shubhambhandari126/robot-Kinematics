Robot Arm Studio

Drive the hand (IK) or the joints (FK), set joint limits, dodge an obstacle, and run a real pick-and-place task.

Two directions of robot motion. Forward kinematics (FK): you set joint angles, the hand ends up wherever that puts it. Inverse kinematics (IK): you pick where the hand should go, the robot solves every joint. Switch modes to feel the difference. Then try joint limits (real motors can't spin freely), an obstacle, and the pick & place task, the actual job robots do.

Why this matters. Pick-and-place is the warehouse task; trajectory tracing is how robots weld or draw; joint limits and obstacle avoidance are what separate a sim from a real deployable arm. This 2D studio runs the same FABRIK inverse-kinematics core that real planners build on, in your browser.
